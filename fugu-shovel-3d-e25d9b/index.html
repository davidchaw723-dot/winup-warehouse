This simulation rebuilds the shovel from the shape ratios measured off the Floating Retort Shovel slides and The Floating Shovel_V2.dwg (the DWG carries no dimensions), scaled to the customer's measured bore. Every figure below marked assumed is our back-calculation from physical constraints, not your number. Please correct them — the model updates directly from these values.

0“Dense in, loose out” (里实口松) — confirm it is a matter of bed height, not density? The 15 Jul technical deck (P05 ③) promises to turn it into four numbers in the PLC + per-charge weighing, but those four numbers are not in the deck.
We first read it as density (compact the inner charges) — which capped the adjustable range at 6.7% and, worse, contradicted the anti-breakage claim the shovel exists to support (P07). Reading it as bed height removes the contradiction: “dense in” = the inner bed sits high (narrow top channel), “loose out” = the bed towards the mouth sits low (wide channel), with density constant at as-poured 1 250 kg/m³ and no compaction anywhere. It also keeps compaction force out of the drive end, so the force trace stays a clean reading of retort deflection. This matches the stepped bed drawn on P10.		 bed height,

0b⚠ P10 contradicts itself — which half is right? The drawing shows four charges filling the full 3 200 mm with no gap. The text says “stage 4 heaps at the same stop position as stage 3”. These cannot both hold: if stage 4 stops where stage 3 does, the material can only heap from ~1 350 mm towards the mouth and the first ~800 mm of the bore is left empty — contradicting the drawing.
We modelled the drawing (full fill), because it is what closes against the 200 kg charge: 4 × 50 kg = 0.160 m³ = 81.2% of the bore volume. Derived stops are 2 701 / 2 139 / 1 348 / 492 mm, i.e. successive gaps of 562 / 791 / 856 mm — stage 3→4 is the largest gap, not “same position”.
